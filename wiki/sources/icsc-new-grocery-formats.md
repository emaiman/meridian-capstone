# ICSC: New grocery formats, store growth and investment signal a sector in motion

**What it is:** a trade-press article by John Egan for ICSC's Commerce + Communities Today, published 2026-04-24. Tier: **Allowed** (public article; no client data). The raw file is an extract made by fetching and summarizing the page, not the verbatim text, so confidence is medium and figures should be checked against the original before use outside the wiki.

## Key points

- The U.S. grocery sector is valued at $912.4 billion (IBISWorld estimate). Grocery-anchored center deals reached nearly $11 billion in 2025, up 42% from 2024.
- Per Consumer Edge, Trader Joe's grew 3% year over year, outperforming the sector by 6 percentage points, while traditional grocers such as Publix and Safeway declined across income groups. Discount grocer growth has plateaued since mid-2025.
- Big players are moving toward differentiation and new formats: Target is repositioning its grocery business toward specialty, Aldi is piloting modular formats, and Walmart and Costco are remodeling and opening stores.
- Gelson's (a Southern California grocer) launched 3,000 to 5,400 sq ft micromarkets paired with EV charging hubs.
- Trend toward specialty and fresh formats, smaller urban footprints, and flexible store designs.
- JLL's Naveen Jaggi: "the days when any grocery store could anchor a center equally well are fading," meaning anchor tenants now differ in performance.
- Investors are active in grocery-anchored real estate in Southern California, for example a Vallarta-anchored center in Los Angeles sold for $100 million.
